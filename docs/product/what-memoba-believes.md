# What Memoba believes

[Teams start from scratch](why-teams-start-from-scratch.md) because finding past work costs too much, and because what they find has lost its reasoning. Memoba is built on a few beliefs about how to change that. Every part of the product comes from one of them.

The product itself is one loop. A person records what happened. Somebody else asks a question in plain words. Memoba reads what the team recorded and answers. The person opens the sources, decides, and later records how it went. Anything that does not serve this loop stays out.

## Asking should be the easy choice

If using a tool takes more effort than skipping it, people skip it, however good it is. So there is no form in front of a question, no taxonomy to learn, and no need to know that a relevant case exists. You write what you are working on, the way you would tell a colleague.

Memoba does the looking. It decides what to search, opens what seems relevant, and comes back with an answer. All of that effort moves from the person to the product.

## Memory is reasoning, not files

A file holds whatever its author needed at the time. The next person needs something else: the problem, what was true then, what was tried, and what actually happened.

That is what a case records. A case is a short account of a real attempt, with its conditions and its outcome. The outcome is achieved, not achieved, or unknown. Unknown is an honest answer, and it stays visible.

A failure deserves the same place as a success, and often a better one. The launch that went wrong teaches more than the one that went well, as long as somebody wrote down why.

Recording has a cost, so Memoba keeps it small. There are two templates, Product/Delivery and CS/Operations, and only the fields a later reader could never reconstruct are required. [How knowledge gets in](../engine/how-knowledge-gets-in.md) covers the details.

## What happened and what is required are different things

A team knows two kinds of things. Cases say what happened. Process documents say what is required now.

Mix them and both get worse. A one-off shortcut starts to read as the rule. A case from before a policy existed gets called a violation. So Memoba keeps the two memories apart everywhere: in how they are stored, in how they are read, and under separate headings in an answer. When a case and a document differ, you see both, and neither is used to judge the other.

Both matter because of what you really want to know: what is different this time. Sometimes the difference is in the request. Sometimes it is in the rules. A price change that worked two years ago may not be allowed today, and past cases alone would not tell you.

## You came for an answer, not a list

A list of search results hands the reading back to you, and the reading is the cost Memoba exists to remove. So Memoba answers, the way a colleague who has been here longer would. It opens with the direct reply, then follows the same shape each time:

- What the process says.
- How similar cases went.
- Where practice and process differ, only when they really do.
- What's different this time.
- Suggested next step.

"What's different this time" has its own heading because it is the most valuable part and the easiest to skip. Past work helps only when you can see where it stops applying.

## Trust comes from checking

Memoba gives no confidence score, because a score would be the model grading itself. Every statement drawn from a source carries a citation you can open, and a citation can only point at a section Memoba actually read.

Reliable does not mean always right. It means you can see where each statement came from, and you can check it faster than you could have found it yourself. The decision stays with you.

## Not knowing is an answer

The fastest way to lose trust is to invent a precedent. When nothing relevant is recorded, Memoba says so and helps from what it does have.

"We have no precedent" and "we could not look" are different statements that lead to different decisions. Memoba keeps them apart. If a source could not be searched, the answer says it was not checked.

## Only people create experience

Memoba's answers are advice, and advice is not experience. An answer never becomes a case, and a suggested step never becomes something the team did. If generated text flowed back into memory, the team's record would slowly fill with things nobody did.

The same rule applies at the door. Memoba can draft a case from your notes, and a person reviews and saves it. What the team remembers is what people chose to record.

## Prove the answer before automating the input

It would be easy to connect every wiki, tracker, and chat history, and fill the product with data on day one. Memoba starts the other way round. Automation makes a working loop cheaper, and it cannot make a loop work. So the answer comes first, and what feeds it comes later.
